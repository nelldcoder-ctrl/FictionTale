### 01 · Create characters

Start with a name and short description. Everything else is optional. Put each character in one **Cast Group** to organize the cast; use tags for details that can apply to many characters. The optional questionnaire offers prompts to help you discover what a character thinks, feels, and chooses. Keep only the answers that help your story.

### 02 · Write stories

Create a story from scratch or use a template. Chapters keep a long draft in order; each chapter can have notes and an in-world time, such as “Day 2” or “Year 412.” The story timeline follows chapters in this one story. FictionTale can point out possible mix-ups in ages, places, or who is present, but it never changes your writing. Use Focus mode to hide extra controls. Version history lets you compare saved drafts or restore an earlier one.

### 03 · Organize a book series

Create a series, then open each book’s details to add it and set its book number. Mark characters and places shared across the books. The **Series Timeline** is a shared list of important events across the whole series; you can note which book and chapter each event belongs to. It sits alongside, rather than replaces, each book’s own chapter timeline.

### 04 · Plan and track your writing

The **Dashboard** shows a story’s word count, chapters, progress, and next tasks. In **Plan**, use scene cards, prompts, and the relationship map when they help. **Relationship History** records how a connection changes at a chapter or story point. A character snapshot records what that character knows, where they are, or who they trust at that point. Give both the same chapter or timeline label to see them together.

**Story timeline:** dates chapters within one story. **Master Chronology:** brings character events, story events, and world events together across the project. Use it to compare what happens and when; it can show in-world order or writing order.

### 05 · Build your setting

Use **Places** for locations. Use **World Entries** for groups, objects, rules of magic or technology, species, languages, and terms. Add tags to make things easier to find.

### 06 · Save and back up your work

The header shows whether your latest changes are **Saved** on this device and whether a backup is due. Open it to reach **Settings → Data & Storage** and export a full backup. Importing a new backup adds a separate project; replacing matching older data asks you first. Your work is not stored online, so keep a backup somewhere separate from this browser and device.

### 07 · Appearance and offline use

Choose a light or dark theme in **Settings → Appearance**. When opened from a secure website or local server, FictionTale can be installed and used without internet. Your writing still stays in this browser on this device; offline use is not an online backup.

### 08 · Draft conversations

Use **Dialogue** to try out a conversation before adding it to your story. Choose who is speaking and note how the conversation feels. Add it to a story when you are ready.

> **Keep your work safe:** your writing is not backed up online. Export a full backup regularly and keep a copy somewhere separate from this browser and device.
